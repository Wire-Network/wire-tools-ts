# Governance handoff rehearsal

Rehearses handing the root `sysio` account from its bootstrap key to the tier-1 node owners, governing it by `sysio.msig`, and handing it on to a council. Run it through `scripts/run-flow.mjs flow-governance-handoff` with a fresh cluster and the canonical heartbeat monitor.

The bootstrap registers no tier-1 owner, so the roster is only the owners the flow registers and each threshold (`floor(2N/3)+1`) follows from their count: 2 of 2 after the first `syncnodes`, 3 of 3 after the handoff, and 3 of 4 once the late owner is synced. The flow checks the roster on chain before each probe that counts against it.

The scenario, in order:

1. The bootstrap created `sysio.nodes` and `sysio.cncl` with `owner` = `sysio@active`, the unsatisfiable `active` = `sysio.null@active`, and 640 bytes of RAM for 21 members.
2. Two tier-1 owners register under seeded keys the flow controls (`flow-governance-handoff/<account>`), each through one `sysio.roa::nodeownreg`, the claim the depot dispatches. The claim creates the account under that key; an account that already exists is rejected. `syncnodes` writes the on-chain tier-1 roster into `sysio.nodes@active` (2 of 2), and the flow checks it against the roster read back from `sysio.roa`.
3. An authority audit (`WireAuthorityAuditTool`, before-handoff mode) enumerates every `sysio.*` account from the chain's trace history and classifies every key, delegation and action link of every permission, and checks `sysio.nodes@active` against the tier-1 roster. Each exception it allows is logged with its reason; anything it cannot classify fails the flow.
4. A third owner registers after that `syncnodes`; `sysio.nodes@active` does not list it. The handoff is one transaction signed by the bootstrap key: `sysio.councl::syncnodes`, then `updateauth` of `sysio@active`, then of `sysio@owner`, both to `sysio.nodes@active`. Afterwards `sysio.nodes@active` is the full roster with the third owner on it, which only the transaction's own `syncnodes` can have written, and that roster is exactly the three flow owners at a threshold of 3. The audit runs again in after-handoff mode, where any key on `sysio` fails it: the bootstrap key alone may now satisfy only the `sysio.andon` panic permissions, and a `sysio@active` action signed by it alone is refused.
5. A probe proposal (a 1 WIRE transfer from `sysio`) with one approval fewer than the threshold is refused by a dry-run `exec`; with the threshold it executes.
6. A fourth owner claims through `BAR.commitNode` over OPP. Before `syncnodes`, `sysio.nodes@active` is still the synced roster: a dry-run `exec` of a proposal the three initial owners approved is accepted, and of one the late owner and two initial owners approved is refused. `syncnodes` makes the roster every flow owner and leaves the threshold at 3 (3 of 4), which the flow checks. Afterwards both proposals are accepted: the late owner's approval now counts, and the three initial owners' approvals still meet the unchanged threshold. The late owner's proposal then executes.
7. **Stand-in for an election.** A real election needs exactly 21 tier-1 owners and is outside this flow. Instead the node-owner msig writes four stand-in members into `sysio.cncl@active` and hands `sysio` to `sysio.cncl@active`. The council's supermajority then governs (one approval short is refused by a dry run). A proposal the node owners approved earlier no longer executes, and a new one they would propose is refused, both read by dry runs, before and after another `syncnodes`. A final audit, given the stand-in council as `sysio.cncl`'s expected members, confirms the retired key's reach.
